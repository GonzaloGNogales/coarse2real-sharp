Abstract

Coarse-to-real (C2R) video generation converts coarse animation, simulation, or game-rendered videos into photorealistic videos while preserving scene layout, camera motion, and dynamics. Training such models requires aligned (coarse, realistic) video pairs, which are expensive to create and confine prior systems to narrow synthetic domains.

We exploit a simple asymmetry: paired videos are hard to obtain, but paired real-to-coarse images are easy to construct with existing image editing and reconstruction tools. From such image pairs we train the DINO-R2C model, which transfers realistic images to coarse images in DINO feature space, and apply it to every frame of ordinary real videos to synthesize pseudo-coarse videos. The C2R# model then learns to generate each real video from its pseudo-coarse counterpart, without any authored (coarse, realistic) video pairs. At inference, the DINO-R2C model is discarded and actual coarse videos drive the C2R# model directly. Training thus scales with real-video corpora rather than with paired synthetic videos.

Method

C2R# is trained without paired coarse and real videos. First, the DINO-R2C model learns from paired realistic and coarse images and is then applied to each frame of real videos, turning them into pseudo-coarse videos. The C2R# model is trained on these pseudo-paired videos: trainable control and fusion adapters feed the pseudo-coarse video into a frozen Wan 2.2 backbone, which learns to produce the original real video. At inference, an actual coarse video conditions C2R# directly and DINO-R2C is no longer needed. Click the figure to enlarge it.

Comparisons

Five examples for each input type, generated from the same input and prompt. Top row: input, C2R# (ours), C2R Original and Editto Global. Bottom row: Editto Sim2Real, Editto Sim2Real (rep.), Wan 2.2 VACE and Wan 2.2 VACE (rep.). "(rep.)" marks our reproductions: the same baselines fine-tuned on our pseudo-paired videos.

In these examples, C2R# (ours) produces more photorealistic results than the baselines while better preserving the structure and motion of the input. C2R Original often changes the scene, Editto Global restyles colors and lighting, Editto Sim2Real often produces misaligned, overly bright outputs that are close to white, and Wan 2.2 VACE tends to keep the input's style, which makes larger domain jumps hard, so it struggles more when the input is very coarse. Training Editto Sim2Real and Wan 2.2 VACE on our pseudo-paired videos (rep.) improves their results in several cases.

Reference-image conditioning

A separate version of C2R# also takes a reference image to guide appearance, in addition to the input video and prompt. A frozen Wan VAE encodes the reference image into extra tokens, and rank-16 attention LoRA adds the guidance while keeping the control and fusion adapters. Each example shows the reference image, the input and the result.

Decoding and re-encoding ablation

The features predicted by DINO-R2C already live in DINO space and could condition C2R# directly. However, they keep hidden cues of the real input video that are absent when an actual coarse video is encoded at inference, a mismatch between training and inference. C2R# therefore decodes each prediction to coarse pixels with the frozen RAE decoder and re-encodes those pixels with the same frozen encoder used at inference.
